**What it is.** The one-word answer to "what is this actor doing right now?"
— expanding, consolidating, hedging, contracting, dormant. Deliberately
qualitative: the axes say what an actor *has* and *moves*; posture says what
it is *trying to do*.

**How we assign it.** Read from the actor's threads and moves — the direction
of [thrust](/metric/thrust/), announced commitments, retreats, hedges — and
kept under standing review rather than computed. A posture starts
provisional, gets confirmed or corrected through the steering loop, and every
posture claim cites the developments it rests on.

**How it relates to the axes.** Posture is the narrative layer the numbers
beg for. Oracle's numbers (buybacks collapsing, capex doubling) *are* a
posture change — rentier turning builder — but the word is asserted, not
derived, because numbers lag intent and management headlines lead it.

**Honest limits.** Posture is the most interpretive claim on the board. It's
also the frame that most rewards attention: a wrong posture is more
informative to correct than a wrong decimal. Treat it as a standing
hypothesis per actor, held to the same receipt discipline as everything else.
