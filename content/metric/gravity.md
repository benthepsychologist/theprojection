title: "Gravity"
weight: 3
unit: "$/yr (flow)"
role: "measured axis"
adopted: "G-SIB substitutability (Basel) · forward linkage · granularity (Gabaix)"
---

**What it is.** The third-party economy that breaks if the actor stops.
Not the actor's own revenue, and not a gross "ecosystem" headline — the
**attributable** annual value that others produce *because* this actor
exists and functions.

**How we calculate it.** Two structural reads, then a dollar estimate:

| component | question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **substitutability** | how replaceable is the service? chokepoint share · switching cost · time-to-replace · number of real alternatives |
| **forward linkage** | how much downstream production draws on this actor's output |
| cross-check: **granularity** | what share of the aggregate does this single actor drive? |

The published figure is an order-of-magnitude estimate with its reasoning
shown, always **value-added, never gross**. Vendor "ecosystem" studies count
total downstream revenue, double-count intermediaries, and skip the
counterfactual — the standard critique of App-Store-style numbers. We deflate
to what's attributable, and say what the dependents would earn without the
actor when we can.

**The canonical example.** Arm books ~$5B of revenue; its instruction set is
in ~99% of smartphones, and Apple alone earns ~$400B a year on Arm-based
silicon. Weight tells you Arm is small. Gravity tells you the world can't
turn it off. The gap between the two axes is the whole reason both exist.

**Confidence, honestly.** Gravity is the lowest-confidence axis by design —
there is no filed document for "who breaks if we stop." Every gravity claim
shows its rationale, and most carry a low-confidence flag. Treat them as
directional coordinates, not audited figures.

**Prior art.** The Basel Committee's G-SIB substitutability score is the
working, published version of this question for banks; forward
linkage/upstreamness is the input-output economics version; Gabaix's
granularity is the macro-volatility version; weaponized interdependence
(Farrell & Newman) is the geopolitical version — dependency that is also a
chokepoint. We borrow the constructs and keep the arithmetic rough.
